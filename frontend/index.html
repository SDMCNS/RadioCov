RF Propagation Physics & Coverage Logic

Physical principles, ITU-R recommendations, and 1,000-ft volumetric cube methodology
1. Core Philosophy Domain-Agnostic Radio Frequency Spectrum Assessment

RadioCov abstracts radio wave propagation completely from domain-specific rules (such as aviation flight rules or cellular cell-site boundaries). Any transceiver in space defined by (Latitude, Longitude, Altitude AMSL) can be evaluated across any frequency band from VHF (30 MHz) through microwave and mmWave (40 GHz). Coverage is discretized into a 3D matrix of 1,000-ft cubes above real physical terrain.

2. Earth Geometry Atmospheric Refraction & Apparent Earth Bulge

Radio waves refract in the Earth's atmosphere due to vertical gradients in temperature, pressure, and humidity. This is modeled using an equivalent Earth radius $R_e$ with a refraction factor $k$:

$$R_e = k \cdot R_0 \quad \text{where } R_0 = 6,371\text{ km (mean Earth radius)}$$

Under standard atmospheric conditions, $k = 4/3 \approx 1.333$ (standard 4/3 Earth model). Across a distance $D$, an obstacle at distance $d_1$ from the transmitter and $d_2$ from the receiver ($d_1 + d_2 = D$) experiences an apparent geometric elevation (Earth bulge):

$$h_{\text{bulge}} = \frac{d_1 \cdot d_2}{2 R_e} \quad \text{(meters)}$$

The effective terrain obstacle height evaluated against the direct ray is:

$$h_{\text{effective}}(d) = h_{\text{DEM}}(d) + h_{\text{bulge}}(d)$$
3. Wave Optics First Fresnel Zone ($F_1$) & Optical vs. Radio LoS

Unlike light, radio waves travel within an ellipsoidal volume between transmitter and receiver. The boundary of the $n$-th Fresnel zone represents paths where the phase length differs by $n \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2}$.

$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} \quad \text{where } c = 299,792,458\text{ m/s (speed of light)}$$
$$F_n = \sqrt{\frac{n \cdot \lambda \cdot d_1 \cdot d_2}{d_1 + d_2}} \quad \implies \quad F_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda \cdot d_1 \cdot d_2}{D}} \quad \text{(meters)}$$

The Fresnel Clearance Ratio $C_r$ compares the vertical clearance $\Delta h = h_{\text{LOS}} - h_{\text{effective}}$ against the 1st Fresnel zone radius:

$$C_r = \frac{\Delta h}{F_1} = \frac{h_{\text{LOS}} - h_{\text{effective}}}{F_1}$$
• Optical LoS: Satisfied when $\Delta h \ge 0$ (direct line of sight clears physical ground).
• Radio LoS (ITU-R criteria): Satisfied when $C_r \ge 0.60$ ($60\%$ of $F_1$ radius is unobstructed). Below $0.60\,F_1$, diffraction attenuation occurs even if optical LoS is clear.
4. Diffraction Physics Knife-Edge Diffraction Attenuation (ITU-R Recommendation P.526)

When terrain encroaches into the 1st Fresnel zone or obstructs the direct line of sight, diffractive signal loss occurs. This is modeled via the dimensionless Fresnel-Kirchhoff diffraction parameter $v$:

$$v = h_{\text{obs}} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda} \left(\frac{1}{d_1} + \frac{1}{d_2}\right)} = - \Delta h \sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda} \left(\frac{1}{d_1} + \frac{1}{d_2}\right)}$$

• $v < -0.78$: Clearance exceeds $0.6\,F_1 \implies$ Diffraction loss $J(v) = 0\text{ dB}$.
• $v = 0$: Grazing line-of-sight obstacle $\implies J(0) \approx 6.02\text{ dB}$ loss.
• $v > 0$: Obstacle penetrates direct LoS $\implies$ Progressive diffraction attenuation:

$$J(v) = 6.9 + 20 \log_{10}\left(\sqrt{(v - 0.1)^2 + 1} + v - 0.1\right) \quad \text{dB}$$
5. Link Budget Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL) & Total Attenuation

The geometric expansion of the wave-front across 3D free space produces Free-Space Path Loss:

$$\text{FSPL}(\text{dB}) = 20 \log_{10}(D) + 20 \log_{10}(f) + 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{4\pi}{c}\right)$$
$$\text{FSPL}(\text{dB}) = 20 \log_{10}(D_{\text{meters}}) + 20 \log_{10}(f_{\text{Hz}}) - 147.55$$

Total path loss combines free-space divergence with terrain diffraction attenuation:

$$L_{\text{total}}(\text{dB}) = \text{FSPL}(\text{dB}) + J(v)(\text{dB})$$
6. Volumetric Grid 1,000-ft Airspace Voxel Cube Architecture

The 3D space surrounding the transmitter is structured into a polar-volumetric voxel lattice:

  • Vertical Resolution: Discrete 1,000 ft layers ($\Delta z = 304.8\text{ m}$) from user-defined $Alt_{\min}$ to $Alt_{\max}$.
  • Azimuthal Radials: Radials spaced uniformly across $[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$ (e.g. 72 radials $= 5^\circ$ angular resolution).
  • Radial Range Increments: Step distance $\Delta r$ (e.g. 2.5 km or 5.0 km) up to maximum range.
  • 3D Cartesian Mapping (Three.js WebGL):
    $$X = r \cdot \sin(\theta), \quad Y = r \cdot \cos(\theta), \quad Z = \text{Alt}_{\text{AMSL}} \cdot s_z$$
  • Horizontal Slicing: Extracts the 2D polygon footprint and coverage percentage at any chosen 1,000-ft layer.
  • Vertical Slicing: Evaluates the full ray profile, Earth curvature, Fresnel envelope, and obstacle clearance along any radial azimuth.
7. Digital Elevation EU-DEM 25m Resolution Terrain Model

Ground elevation is extracted from the European Copernicus EU-DEM v1.1 GeoTIFF raster dataset at 25-meter horizontal resolution (projected in ETRS89-LAEA / EPSG:3035). Coordinate conversions use ellipsoidal geodetic transforms:

$$(\text{Latitude, Longitude})_{\text{WGS84}} \xleftrightarrow{\text{PyProj / GDAL}} (X, Y)_{\text{EPSG:3035}} \longrightarrow h_{\text{DEM}}(\text{meters})$$

Ray profiles sample terrain elevation via windowed sub-array reads with LRU caching to evaluate hundreds of thousands of path samples with millisecond responsiveness.

Standardized to ITU-R P.526-15, ITU-R P.834, and EU-DEM 25m.
